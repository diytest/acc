Impact Testing of Transport

ISO 6487:2002 "Road vehicles -- Measurement techniques in impact tests -- Instrumentation"  sets out a number of requirements for the measurement and recording system used in impact testing of vehicles. 

"The following equipment shall be available when conducting the test:
a) two accelerometers with a minimum amplitude range of 200g,
a lower frequency limit of no more than 1Hz,
an upper frequency limit of at least 3000Hz.

b) an analog-to-digital data acquisition system capable of recording shock disturbances as an "acceleration - time" graph, at a minimum sampling rate of 1000Hz.
The data acquisition system must include an analog low-pass filter to suppress interference, with a cutoff frequency of at least 200Hz and at most 20% of the sampling rate, and a minimum rolloff of 40dB/octave".



Among the commercially available MEMS accelerometers, high-frequency single-axis accelerometers are suitable for this task with a measured acceleration range of ±250g in a frequency range of 0...22000Hz.
Low-frequency MEMS-accelerometers are significantly cheaper, but their frequency range is limited, at best, to 1600Hz (whereas at least 3000Hz is required)..



The requirement to use an analog low-pass filter (LPF) with a rolloff of at least 40dB/octave.
On the internet you can find literature justifying the need to use specifically analog low-pass filters for this task - not digital filters implemented on a DSP or on switched-capacitor chips, and even less so filters implemented in software on a computer.

It is known that a Chebyshev filter provides the steepest frequency-response rolloff, but it also introduces significant phase distortion, which would distort the measurement results.
Acceptable in terms of phase characteristics is a Butterworth filter, which is also mentioned in ISO 6487:2002 .
An 8th-order analog Butterworth low-pass filter can provide a frequency-response rolloff of no worse than 40dB per octave.

The impact measurement system includes

two single-axis accelerometers

with a measured acceleration range of ±250g and a frequency range of 0...22000Hz

 
each accelerometer is housed in a separate enclosure,
on the left - the enclosure with the cable connected, on the right without the cable..
The accelerometer enclosure also contains a buffer amplifier that lets the signal be transmitted over a cable several meters long to a low-impedance load, and a voltage regulator.

Each of the two accelerometers is connected by a separate cable to the data processing/recording unit.

The data processing/recording unit contains a two-channel analog filter and an analog-to-digital converter board with memory - a separate signal-processing channel for each of the two accelerometers.

analog filter

The two-channel active 8th-order analog Butterworth low-pass filter is built using high-quality operational amplifiers, with precision capacitors and resistors, which provides temperature and time stability of the filter's frequency response (FR).


 




 Board of the two-channel analog low-pass filter, and part of the housing of the recording unit.
 

 The data processed by the filters is fed to the ADC inputs.
 






Analog-to-digital converter (ADC)

In the second part of the housing of the data processing/recording unit the board of the 8-channel ADC
is installed. The ADC is 12-bit, with 512KB of memory for the recorded data,
The ADC connects with a cable to the USB port of a computer (laptop, netbook).

The ADC's operating mode (sampling rate, measurement time, measurement start) is selected by the operator in the computer application.
During operation,  4 ADC channels can be used - two channels can record the unfiltered signals directly from the accelerometers, and the other two - the signals processed by the filter.
The converted data is recorded into a memory chip on the ADC board, and once the measurement is complete, this data is transferred from memory over the USB cable to the computer, where it is saved to files and displayed as graphs.

System power

is supplied from the computer's USB port. No additional power sources or cables are required.

Using a low-pass filter with a normalized frequency response makes it possible to obtain identical results when measuring with accelerometers of different frequency characteristics (provided that the low-pass filter's cutoff frequency is much lower than the upper cutoff frequencies of the accelerometers used).
