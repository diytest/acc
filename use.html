Vehicle accident: methods for determining a vehicle's speed in a frontal impact with a pedestrian

A USB accelerometer with memory is installed in the crash-test dummy used  in tests of a vehicle striking a pedestrian.
The results recorded by the accelerometer are used to compare methods for estimating a vehicle's speed before it strikes a pedestrian.

Clicking the image will take you to a video clip showing the accelerometer's installation.
Link to the full video, posted by its authors on Youtube: Pedestrian impact, determining vehicle speed

Impact loading

To obtain the results described in the Kuban paragliders' publication
"A study of the protective properties of protectors for paragliding harnesses."
MEMS accelerometers with analog outputs were used, measuring accelerations up to ±35g in the low-frequency range (0...400Hz), together with an 8-channel ADC board with 512KB of memory. The use of inexpensive low-frequency  MEMS accelerometer chips in these studies is dictated by the nature of the impacts - they can be described as "soft" impacts, since they are damped by protectors that differ in design and properties.

A comparison of results recorded by low-frequency and high-frequency accelerometers during "hard" impacts from a hammer striking concrete and brick walls is given in the presentation strike_concrete.pps
The graphs clearly demonstrate how the shape of the impact is distorted by an insufficiently fast (low-frequency) accelerometer compared with a recording made by a high-frequency accelerometer

BALANCING function

 The USB accelerometers have gained the ability to record a pulse signal. The signal source is connected to the Go and GROUND contacts on the USB accelerometer board.

This function lets you tie an acceleration reading to some marker; it's analogous to the device used when balancing rotating objects - motor rotors, wheels, and so on.

The graph records the acceleration along the Z axis (red graph) that occurs at the handlebar when a bicycle wheel spins freely because of  that wheel's imbalance.

A magnetic sensor from a bicycle odometer was used as the pulse sensor. When the magnet, mounted on one of the wheel's spokes, passes near the sensor, the sensor's resistance drops sharply; no additional parts were needed to connect it to the USB accelerometer, and an LED, connected together with a series resistor to the same contacts on the accelerometer, blinked, indicating each time the magnet passed the sensor.

The black graph is the pulses from the bicycle odometer sensor.

For balancing purposes, the ability to display degrees along the horizontal axis was added. The intervals between the edges of neighboring pulses are evenly filled with degrees from 0 to 360, and moving the cursor along the graph in the status bar also shows the corresponding degree value, which lets you precisely determine the direction of the imbalance. The blue oval marks the cursor's position, and the corresponding value on the status bar is 191°

Evaluating parameters of mechanical structures (RS-485 accelerometer)


 


Oscillations of the pipeline following a calibrated excitation along the Z axis
 The X and Z axes are shown, since they produced a significant response.

 The parameters of the mechanical structure obtained through processing are the resonant  oscillation frequency and damping.






 Tracking how these change over time makes it possible to estimate the structure's wear, determine whether it can continue  to be used, and give recommendations for reinforcing or replacing it.
