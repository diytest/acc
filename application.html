Signal processing in the application

Automatic offset compensation (zero setting)


MEMS accelerometers are sensitive to gravity, so if one of the sensitivity axes of the accelerometer is positioned vertically, the corresponding channel will show a signal proportional to the acceleration of free fall (1g). When the accelerometer is used to measure tilt, information about the magnitude of the gravity vector's projections on the sensitivity axes of the accelerometer is the main quantity of interest; when measuring vibration, on the other hand, a constant offset is undesirable.

The application lets you first perform an offset measurement, and then automatically compensate for it in subsequent measurements.

Fix the accelerometer in its working position, try to ensure there are no vibrations, and press the "Calculate for selected channels" button

The compensation will remain correct as long as the orientation of the accelerometer in space (the angle of tilt relative to vertical) stays unchanged.
If the initial orientation of the accelerometer changes, repeat the offset calculation before performing measurements.
It is even better to perform this procedure before a series of measurements - this way you compensate for any change in offset caused by a change in temperature.

Exponential smoothing of signals




The application lets you remove noise from the graph by smoothing the signal. The user can choose the weight with which smoothing is performed, on the "Chart" panel (see picture). The higher the weight, the stronger the smoothing.
The original graph is shown at the top. Below is the same graph after smoothing with a weight of 50


If the smoothing option is enabled, the calculation of the spectrum, trend, and signal magnitude is performed on the smoothed signals.
When measuring impact accelerations or at a high signal frequency, using this option will smooth out the signal peaks and reduce the amplitude.
So use smoothing with caution.
Choose the optimal weight for smoothing, checking the amplitude before and after smoothing.

Calculating and displaying the signal magnitude.

The signals in the x, y, z channels  of a three-axis accelerometer are proportional to the projections of the acceleration vector on the X, Y, Z axes,
the magnitude (length) of the acceleration vector can be reconstructed from its projections on the axes.
In the picture,  the acceleration vector is shown as a green arrow, the red segments are the vector's projections on the X, Y, Z axes.

The formula for calculating the acceleration magnitude: |a|=(x²+y²+z²)½. It follows from the formula that the magnitude is always positive.

If the permissible acting acceleration is specified for an object without reference to specific axes, it is precisely the acceleration magnitude that is calculated to assess the acting acceleration.

The acceleration magnitude is used, for example, in an anti-seismic emergency protection or warning system: if the magnitude of acceleration exceeds a threshold level, the protection or warning system is activated (for example, about an earthquake).



On the graph, colored lines show the accelerations along the X, Y, Z axes, and the black line shows the acceleration magnitude.

In the bottom right corner of the picture, on the status bar the time and acceleration values corresponding to the position of the mouse pointer (arrow) on the graph are shown.
To hide/show these values, click on the status bar with the mouse and move the mouse pointer onto the graph.

Let's check the calculation of the acceleration magnitude at time 0.12s using these values: |a|=((-7.38)²+764,07²+(-7983,95)²)½ = 8020,43

The application provides the ability to choose the channels used to calculate the magnitude (see the "Chart" panel/"Calculate MAGNITUDE |a| by channels" panel opened over the graph).

Sometimes the magnitude calculated from a single channel is useful: in that case a negative signal simply changes sign, and the resulting signal is easier to compare with a threshold level.

Determining the time interval, the corresponding frequency, and the difference in acceleration values from the graph


To determine the time interval and the difference in acceleration values between two points on the graph, for example, between two peaks of the signal on the Z axis, move the mouse pointer to one peak and left-click - the first marker will appear (a red diamond),
then move the mouse pointer to the second peak, click again, a second marker will appear, and the status bar at the bottom center will display the value of the time interval dT between the placed markers and the frequency f corresponding to that time interval, as well as the difference in acceleration values dA.

In the example shown, the interval selected  corresponds to one period of the damped oscillation, 175.31ms, and this period corresponds to a frequency of 5.70Hz.
Before copying to the clipboard or saving the graph as a picture, you can hide the markers by unchecking the "Mark" name on the panel for selecting which channels are shown on the graph (X, Y, Z, |a|, Mark).

Signal trend


Calculating the trend lets you estimate the decay of free oscillations of a structure (or its quality factor - the parameter inverse to decay).

The upper graph shows oscillations of a swinging platform decaying according to an exponential law.

The trend is calculated using the least squares method
.
The result is a red straight trend line on the lower graph and its mathematical representation in the form a = q * t + C in the top right corner of that graph:
 a = -0,001695 * t + 4,779371

The coefficient q = -0.001695, which determines the slope of the trend line, is of interest.
How is it used?

To determine the amplitude of free oscillations at a given time  t, given a known amplitude Ao at time to=0, we use the formula:
 A = Ao * exp(qt)
For the upper graph, Ao=280cm/s², q=-0.001695; at the 320th second the amplitude should drop to
A = 280 * exp(-0,001695 * 320) = 163cm/s², which can be confirmed from the upper graph (see the amplitude at 320s).

Documenting

Copying/saving the graph, spectrum, trend


By hovering the mouse pointer over any button, you will get a tooltip about its functions. Below the graph you can see a tooltip that appears when you hover the mouse pointer over the graph-copy button.

If briefly pressed, the button to the left below the graph will save the graph to the clipboard, which can then be pasted, for example, into an MS Word document.

If this button is held for more than a second, after releasing it the application will prompt you to save the graph to a wmf file (Windows Metafile). An image in this format can also be pasted into a document, and it also opens automatically for viewing with Windows tools.

A button with the same functions is located to the left above the spectrum and trend graphs - they can also be copied to the clipboard or saved to a file.

Copying file information and the file comment to the clipboard

When the comment-copy button is pressed, the comment text and data-file information - file name, creation time, and path - are saved to the clipboard. This information can also be pasted into an MS Word document





You only need to perform copy and paste twice, using the application's tools, to get the report shown in the picture on the left in MS Word.

Here there are 3 lines of text - the comment saved in the file,
the "XYZ" line is generated automatically and indicates the recorded channels.
Next comes the full file name and its creation time.

Write detailed comments before saving the file!


When plotting the spectrum and trend, the relevant results are automatically sent to the clipboard in a form convenient for pasting into a report along with the spectrum or trend graphs - see the Help file for details.

Exporting results to other applications

The application lets you save all measurement results or a fragment of them to a text file or to a file in CSV format (this is also a text format with defined delimiter characters).
Import and export of CSV files is supported by many engineering packages and spreadsheet applications, for example, MS Excel, ANSYS, LabVIEW, MatLab, OpenOffice.org Calc, KSpread, Google Docs



Open a file, select a fragment on the graph that you want to save to a txt or csv file



Press the data-save button






In the "Save file" window that opens, enter a file name and choose the format of the file to be saved.

Let's choose the csv format. Let's save the file.





If the saved file is opened in MS Excel, the Excel sheet will contain all the necessary information about the saved data:

Row 1: file name, the time the measurement recording started
Row 2: channel #    offsets C by channel    conversion coefficient K by channel   formula for computing the result from ADC units
Rows 3, 4, 5: the values of the listed parameters that were in effect when the file was recorded.

Rows 6, 7: axis names

Row 8: comment

Rows starting from 10 - the measurement results for the fragment selected on the graph

Applying the formula and the parameters C and K:
the offset C - a parameter obtained as a result of zero setting (see above, Automatic offset compensation); K - the accelerometer's calibration coefficients.

If needed, instead of the VALUES of the measurement results, you can display the raw data transmitted by the accelerometer - ADC UNITS - on the graph. The output mode is selected on the "Chart" panel

Then you will be able, for example, to change the offset C and present the graph in third-party applications so that, for example, the data for each axis is arranged one above the other - X, below it Y, and below that Z.
Or you may be able to compensate for an offset that you forgot to calculate before the measurement.

The values of the C parameters for the three channels let you determine the orientation of the accelerometer; for example, if the accelerometer's Z axis is positioned vertically, the axis values will be roughly as follows:
X  Y  Z
-1 2 250

Values in the Excel sheet (-170  -186  -1) indicate that the Z axis was positioned horizontally, with X and Y at roughly -45°  to the vertical axis.

Saving to a TXT file gives the same results, but they are presented in a way that is more convenient for pasting into a report or for viewing in Notepad.

When plotting the spectrum and trend, the results are automatically transferred to the clipboard in a form convenient for export to Excel.

For the spectrum - this is the frequency and value of the spectral component with the highest level, and if the "Spectrum to clipboard" option is selected in the spectrum window, the values of all spectral components are also transferred to the clipboard

For the trend - the coefficient q, which determines the slope angle of the trend, and the offset C (the parameters for the formula describing the trend: a = q * t + C)

Also transferred to the clipboard are the channel, the starting and ending point of the graph fragment from which the spectrum or trend was calculated, and the file name.

The transferred parameters are enough to demonstrate the calculation again, if the original file has been kept.

Searching for files by comments


If, before saving a file, you don't skip entering a unique meaningful comment, you will later easily find it among the many files stored in the same folder.

Open any file with the extension axd in the folder you are interested in, then press the "View comments" button in the "File" menu.

From all the axd files in the folder, the application will extract their comments and place them in a window.

Each comment is preceded by the file name, for example,
=v=KK.axd=v=

The sequence of three characters =v= marks the file name; it should not be used when writing comments.

You can search within the comments for a fragment of text - type it into the "SEARCH" box. By pressing the "Search" button, you can find all comments that contain the text you entered.

Double-clicking with the mouse on the text of a comment will open the file that the comment belongs to
or
click on the comment with the mouse and press the "Open file" button.


Write detailed comments before saving the file!
